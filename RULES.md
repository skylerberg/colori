# Colori — Rules

## Overview

Colori is a deck-building game for 2–5 players about dyeing materials and selling to buyers. Players draft pigment, material and special cards, trash them to store colors on a color wheel, mix colors, and sell to buyers for points. The game lasts 7 rounds. The player with the most points wins.

## Components

### Color Wheel

The color wheel has 12 colors arranged in a circle:

**Red** → Vermilion → **Orange** → Amber → **Yellow** → Chartreuse → **Green** → Teal → **Blue** → Ultramarine → **Purple** → Fuchsia → (back to Red)

- **Primary colors**: Red, Yellow, Blue
- **Secondary colors**: Orange (between Red and Yellow), Green (between Yellow and Blue), Purple (between Blue and Red)
- **Tertiary colors**: Vermilion (between Red and Orange), Amber (between Orange and Yellow), Chartreuse (between Yellow and Green), Teal (between Green and Blue), Ultramarine (between Blue and Purple), Fuchsia (between Purple and Red)

Each player has their own color wheel that tracks how many of each color they have stored. The middle of the wheel holds the player's bowls, and its edge counts their boats — see Bowls and Boats below.

### Materials Wheel

The materials wheel next to the color wheel tracks the three stored materials: **Textiles**, **Ceramics** and **Paintings**. The gondola in its middle counts the player's boats.

### Cards

Every card has **top actions** at the top, which resolve when the card is trashed, and — for material, pigment and special cards — something it gives when **workshopped**, printed on the lower half.

**Starter Pigment Cards** (starting decks only, 1 of each per player):
- Red — 1 Red | Top action: Workshop ×2
- Yellow — 1 Yellow | Top action: Workshop ×2
- Blue — 1 Blue | Top action: Workshop ×2

**Pigment Cards** (draft deck, 3 copies each, except 2 of each tertiary):
- Primary pigments — 2 colors of one primary + 1 color of a different primary | Top action: Mix Colors ×2
- Secondary pigments — 1 secondary color + 1 adjacent primary | Top action: Sell to a Buyer
- Tertiary pigments — 1 color of a tertiary color | Top action: Workshop ×3

**The Primaries Card** (draft deck, 6 copies):
An ordinary pigment card carrying one of each primary, so workshopping it stores a Red, a Yellow and a Blue. | Top action: Workshop ×2

**Material Cards** (starting decks, 1 of each per player; plus the Imports cards in the draft deck):
Starting deck materials store 1 material when workshopped. Imports cards store a material and a color, or two different materials.
- Ceramics | Top action: Sell to a Buyer
- Canvas (stores Paintings) | Top action: Sell to a Buyer
- Fabric (stores Textiles) | Top action: Sell to a Buyer

**Special Cards** (draft deck, 5 copies each; plus Chalk as a starter card):
Each special card has a top action plus a workshop ability that triggers when the card is chosen during Workshop resolution. See the card reference below.

**Buyers** (single deck):
Each buyer is a tradesperson who wants a set of colors and, for most trades, one or two stored materials. They are shuffled into a single deck. Completing a buyer's order is worth 2–5 points.

Buyers are not sold to straight out of the display. Each round, before drafting, players claim buyers into their own **buyers** section, and for the rest of that round a player can only sell to the buyers sitting there.

### Bowls and Boats

Two things a player can hold besides colors, materials and gold coins:

- A **bowl** stands in for a Red, Yellow or Blue the player's color wheel lacks, whenever a primary would be spent: as an input to a mix, or in a buyer's color cost.
- A **boat** is a wild material. One boat stands in for a material a buyer requires that the player has not stored. Shipwrights also ask for a boat outright.

What is asked for is always spent first; bowls and boats only cover what is missing. Each bowl and each boat still held at the end of the game is worth **half a point**.

### Decks

- **Draft Deck**: 100 cards — 48 pigment cards, 6 Primaries cards, 21 Imports cards and 25 special cards. Trashed cards go to the **trash**; when the draft deck runs out, shuffle the trash back in.
- **Buyer Deck**: Contains all buyers. 7 buyers are displayed face-up at all times, replaced from the deck as soon as one is claimed.
- **Personal Deck**: Each player has their own deck that they draw from and build over the course of the game. There is no discard pile: cards return to the deck by being placed on the **bottom** when a turn ends, so a card you put down will not come back up until everything above it has been drawn.

## Setup

1. Each player receives a starting personal deck: 1 Red, 1 Yellow, 1 Blue, 1 Ceramics, 1 Canvas, 1 Fabric, 1 Chalk. Shuffle it. The physical game has six of these decks, one per character; they differ only in the portrait in the corner, which makes a deck easy to reassemble after a game.
2. Each player starts with 1 Red, 1 Yellow, and 1 Blue already stored on their color wheel.
3. Each player starts with 1 gold coin and 1 boat.
4. Each player draws the top 5 cards of their personal deck into their workshop.
5. Place the pigment, Imports and special cards into the draft deck. Shuffle it.
6. Shuffle the buyer deck and reveal 7 buyers face-up in the buyer display.

## Player Area

Each player has:
- **Personal Deck** (face down)
- **Buyers** (face up — the buyers this player may sell to)
- **Workshop** (face up along the bottom of the board, visible to all players)
- **Top Cards** (face up along the top of the board — the cards drafted this round, plus any lifted out of the workshop)
- **Color Wheel** (tracks stored colors)
- **Materials Wheel** (tracks Textiles, Ceramics and Paintings)
- **Gold Coins** (count toward final score)
- **Bowls** and **Boats** (wilds; half a point each at the end)
- **Completed Orders** (scored buyers, kept face down in a separate area)

## Round Structure

The game is played over **7 rounds**. Each round has three phases: Buyers, Draft and Action. The starting player rotates each round: player `(round - 1) % numPlayers` claims first in the Buyers Phase.

As a round opens, every player collects income:

| Round | Income |
|-------|--------|
| 4 | 1 gold coin |
| 5 | 1 boat |
| 6 | 1 gold coin |
| 7 | 1 gold coin, 1 boat and 1 bowl |

### 1. Buyers Phase

Players claim buyers into their buyers section.

Buyer slots per player:

| Round | Slots |
|-------|-------|
| 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 2 |
| 3–7 | 3 |

Starting with the starting player and going round-robin in turn order, each player with an empty slot claims **one** card, and this repeats until nobody has an empty slot left. A slot only needs filling if it is empty — a buyer that was not sold last round stays where it is.

To claim, take either:
- one of the 7 face-up buyers, which is **replaced from the deck immediately**, so the next player also chooses from a full display; or
- the **top card of the buyer deck**, unseen.

If both the display and the deck are empty there is nothing to claim, and the slot stays empty.

### 2. Draft Phase

1. Deal 4 cards from the draft deck to each player as a draft hand (these are private — other players cannot see them).
2. Each player simultaneously picks 1 card from their hand and places it face-down among their top cards.
3. Once everyone has picked, the picks are turned face-up and stay that way: from then on everyone can see what each player has picked in every finished pick round, while the card being picked now stays hidden.
4. Players pass their remaining cards to the left.
5. Repeat steps 2–4 until each hand is down to its last card, after 3 picks. Each player takes the last card passed to them, so everyone drafts 4 cards and nothing is left over.

**Local multiplayer note**: Since all players share one device, a "Pass device to [player name]" screen is shown between picks to keep draft hands private.

### 3. Action Phase

Everyone takes their turn at the same time. Nothing you do on your turn touches anyone else's cards, colors, materials or buyers, so there is no turn order: you can watch the others play, but nothing they do changes what you can do. The round ends once everyone has ended their turn.

On your turn, each of your **top cards** is decided one of two ways:

- **Use its top action**, then trash the card. A card with two top actions resolves them in the order printed.
- **Keep it.** The card goes on top of your personal deck, to be drawn back into your workshop and workshopped next round.

You may use or keep your top cards in any order, one at a time. A top action must be fully resolved, including any abilities it pushes onto the stack, before another card is decided.

You may end your turn whenever you like, without deciding every card. Anything still waiting on you is set aside: an ability you have not answered is not resolved, and every top card you have not decided is kept — or, if you say so when ending the turn, trashed without using its top action.

**Spending gold coins.** Between top actions — whenever no ability is waiting on you — you may spend **1 gold coin** for any of:

| Cost | Buys |
|------|------|
| 1 gold coin | Workshop ×1 |
| 1 gold coin | Mix Colors ×1 |
| 1 gold coin | Sell to a Buyer |

The purchase resolves like any other ability. You may do this as often as you have gold coins. A gold coin is worth a point at the end of the game, so spending one always costs you a point.

Buying a sale does not conjure it out of nothing: it still spends the material and the colors the buyer asks for, exactly as a drafted Sell to a Buyer would. What a gold coin buys is the *opportunity* — a sale in a round where nothing you drafted lets you make one.

You cannot buy something that would do nothing: a sale needs an affordable buyer, Workshop needs a card in your workshop, and Mix Colors needs two colors on your color wheel that can be mixed.

At the end of your turn:
- Every card in your workshop, including the ones already used this round, is shuffled together and placed on the **bottom** of your personal deck.
- Then you draw from your personal deck until your workshop holds 5 cards again. Your workshop is topped up rather than dealt, so what you kept is part of it. If the personal deck runs out you draw fewer cards — there is nothing to reshuffle.

All player information (workshop, top cards, color wheel, materials wheel, gold coins) is public during the action phase.

## Abilities

A card's top actions resolve when the card is trashed. Abilities are resolved using a **stack (LIFO)** — if an ability triggers further abilities, they are pushed onto the stack and the most recently pushed ability resolves next.

### Workshop ×N

Choose up to N cards from your **workshop**. You may skip Workshop entirely (choosing 0 cards).

For each chosen card:
- **Material card**: Store all materials listed on the card (e.g., 2x Ceramics, or 1 Ceramics + 1 Paintings). If the card has a color, also store that color on your color wheel. The card is used and stays in the workshop.
- **Pigment card**: Store ALL of its colors on your color wheel. The card is used and stays in the workshop.
- **Special card**: Consumes 1 pick. The card's workshop abilities are pushed onto the ability stack, and any remaining Workshop picks carry over (pushed below the workshop abilities on the stack). The card is used and stays in the workshop.

### Mix Colors ×N

Perform up to N mix operations on your color wheel. You may stop early. For each mix:
1. Choose two colors that are exactly **2 positions apart** (e.g., Red and Orange, Yellow and Green). Each must be on your color wheel, or be a primary you have a bowl to stand in for.
2. Remove one of each from your color wheel, spending a bowl for any primary you lack.
3. Add one of the color **between** them to your color wheel.

Complete mixing pairs (two inputs → one output):
| Input 1 | Input 2 | Output |
|---------|---------|--------|
| Red | Orange | Vermilion |
| Orange | Yellow | Amber |
| Yellow | Green | Chartreuse |
| Green | Blue | Teal |
| Blue | Purple | Ultramarine |
| Purple | Red | Fuchsia |
| Red | Yellow | Orange |
| Yellow | Blue | Green |
| Blue | Red | Purple |

### Lift a Card

Choose one card from your **workshop** that has not been used this turn and lift it out of the bottom of your board onto your top cards. A card already used, whose colors and materials have been stored, cannot be lifted. If every card in your workshop has been used, or it is empty, the ability fizzles.

The lifted card is now an ordinary top card. You may use its top action later in your turn, exactly as with a card you drafted; triggered abilities are pushed onto the ability stack, so chain reactions are possible. Or you may keep it, and it goes on top of your deck like any other kept card.

### Sell to a Buyer

You **must** sell to one of your own buyers if you are able to. If you cannot afford any of them, the ability fizzles (nothing happens). The shared display cannot be sold to — cards must be claimed in the buyers phase first.

To sell to a buyer:
1. Choose a buyer from your **buyers** section.
2. Spend the stored materials the card asks for, with a boat standing in for each one you lack. A Shipwright also takes a boat of its own.
3. Pay the color cost by removing the required colors from your color wheel, spending a bowl for each primary you lack.
4. The buyer goes face down onto your **Completed Orders**.
5. The buyer slot it occupied stays empty until the next buyers phase refills it.

### Gain Gold Coins ×N

Gain N gold coins. Gold coins count toward your final score, and can be spent during your turn — see the Action Phase.

### Any Secondary

Choose one of the three secondary colors (Orange, Green, or Purple) and add it to your color wheel. This ability never fizzles.

### Gain Bowls ×N

Gain N bowls. See Bowls and Boats above.

### Gain a Boat

Gain 1 boat. See Bowls and Boats above.

## End of Game

The game ends when **round 7** is completed.

Each player's score = the points on their Completed Orders + 1 point for each gold coin + ½ point for each bowl and each boat they still hold. The player with the highest score wins. If tied, the player with the most Completed Orders wins. If still tied, the player with the most colors (total count) on their color wheel wins. If still tied, those players share the victory.
