# Colori — Rules

## Overview

Colori is a deck-building game for 2–5 players about dyeing materials and selling to sell cards. Players draft dye, material, and action cards, trash them to store colors on a color wheel, mix colors, and sell to sell cards worth points. The game lasts 7 rounds. The player with the most points wins.

## Components

### Color Wheel

The color wheel has 12 colors arranged in a circle:

**Red** → Vermilion → **Orange** → Amber → **Yellow** → Chartreuse → **Green** → Teal → **Blue** → Ultramarine → **Purple** → Fuchsia → (back to Red)

- **Primary colors**: Red, Yellow, Blue
- **Secondary colors**: Orange (between Red and Yellow), Green (between Yellow and Blue), Purple (between Blue and Red)
- **Tertiary colors**: Vermilion (between Red and Orange), Amber (between Orange and Yellow), Chartreuse (between Yellow and Green), Teal (between Green and Blue), Ultramarine (between Blue and Purple), Fuchsia (between Purple and Red)

Each player has their own color wheel that tracks how many of each color they have stored.

### Cards

**Starter Dye Cards** (starting decks only, 1 of each per player):
- Red — 1 Red color | Trash: Workshop ×2
- Yellow — 1 Yellow color | Trash: Workshop ×2
- Blue — 1 Blue color | Trash: Workshop ×2

**Dye Cards** (draft deck, 3 copies each):
- Primary dyes — 2 colors of one primary + 1 color of a different primary | Trash: Mix Colors ×2
- Secondary dyes — 1 secondary color + 1 adjacent primary | Trash: Sell
- Tertiary dyes — 1 color of a tertiary color | Trash: Workshop ×3

**Material Cards** (starting decks, 1 of each per player; plus the Imports cards in the draft deck):
Starting deck materials gain 1 material when workshopped. Imports cards gain a material and a color, or two different materials.
- Ceramics | Trash: Sell
- Canvas (gains Paintings) | Trash: Sell
- Fabric (gains Textiles) | Trash: Sell

**Action Cards** (draft deck, 5 copies each; plus Chalk as a starter card):
Each action card has a trash ability plus workshop abilities that trigger when the card is chosen during Workshop resolution. See the card reference below.

**Sell Cards** (single deck):
Each sell card is a buyer: a tradesperson who wants a set of colors and, for most trades, one or two stored materials. All sell cards are shuffled into a single deck. Each sell card is worth 2–5 points.

Sell cards are not completed straight out of the display. Each round, before drafting, players claim cards into their own **buyers** section, and for the rest of that round a player can only sell to the cards sitting there.

### Wild Primaries and Boats

Two things a player can hold besides colors, materials and ducats:

- A **wild primary** stands in for a Red, Yellow or Blue the player's wheel lacks, whenever a primary would be spent: as an input to a mix, or in a sell card's color cost.
- A **boat** is a wild material. One boat stands in for a material a sell card requires that the player has not stored. Shipwrights also ask for a boat outright.

What is asked for is always spent first; wild primaries and boats only cover what is missing. Each wild primary and each boat still held at the end of the game is worth **half a point**.

### Decks

- **Draft Deck**: 100 cards — 54 dye cards, 21 Imports cards and 25 action cards. Trashed cards go to the **trash**; when the draft deck runs out, shuffle the trash back in.
- **Sell Card Deck**: Contains all sell cards. 7 sell cards are displayed face-up at all times, replaced from the deck as soon as one is claimed.
- **Personal Deck**: Each player has their own deck that they draw from and build over the course of the game. There is no discard pile: cards return to the deck by being placed on the **bottom** at the end of a round, so a card you put down will not come back up until everything above it has been drawn.

## Setup

1. Each player receives a starting personal deck: 1 Red, 1 Yellow, 1 Blue, 1 Ceramics, 1 Canvas, 1 Fabric, 1 Chalk. Shuffle it. The physical game has six of these decks, one per character; they differ only in the portrait in the corner, which makes a deck easy to reassemble after a game.
2. Each player starts with 1 Red, 1 Yellow, and 1 Blue already stored on their color wheel.
3. Each player starts with 1 ducat and 1 boat.
4. Place the dye, Imports, and action cards into the draft deck. Shuffle it.
5. Shuffle the sell card deck and reveal 7 sell cards face-up in the sell card display.

## Player Area

Each player has:
- **Personal Deck** (face down)
- **Buyers** (face up — the sell cards this player may sell to)
- **Workshop** (face up, visible to all players)
- **Drafted Cards** (face up after drafting)
- **Color Wheel** (tracks stored colors)
- **Stored Materials** (tracks Textiles, Ceramics, and Paintings counts)
- **Ducats** (count toward final score)
- **Wild Primaries** and **Boats** (wilds; half a point each at the end)
- **Completed Sell Cards** (scored sell cards, kept face down in a separate area)

## Round Structure

The game is played over **7 rounds**. Each round has four phases. The starting player rotates each round: player `(round - 1) % numPlayers` claims first in the Buyers Phase.

At the start of the later rounds, every player collects income:

| Round | Income |
|-------|--------|
| 4 | 1 ducat |
| 5 | 1 boat |
| 6 | 1 ducat |
| 7 | 1 ducat, 1 boat and 1 wild primary |

### 1. Draw Phase

Each player draws from their personal deck until their workshop holds 5 cards. Cards carried over from the previous round's draft pool are already there, so keeping a card costs you a fresh draw. If the personal deck runs out you draw fewer cards — there is nothing to reshuffle.

Workshop cards are face-up and visible to all players.

### 2. Buyers Phase

Players claim sell cards into their buyers section.

Buyer slots per player:

| Round | Slots |
|-------|-------|
| 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 2 |
| 3–7 | 3 |

Starting with the starting player and going round-robin in turn order, each player with an empty slot claims **one** card, and this repeats until nobody has an empty slot left. A slot only needs filling if it is empty — a buyer that was not sold last round stays where it is.

To claim, take either:
- one of the 7 face-up sell cards, which is **replaced from the deck immediately**, so the next player also chooses from a full display; or
- the **top card of the sell card deck**, unseen.

If both the display and the deck are empty there is nothing to claim, and the slot stays empty.

### 3. Draft Phase

1. Deal 4 cards from the draft deck to each player as a draft hand (these are private — other players cannot see them).
2. Each player simultaneously picks 1 card from their hand and places it face-down in their drafted cards area.
3. Once everyone has picked, the picks are turned face-up and stay that way: from then on everyone can see what each player has drafted in every finished pick round, while the card being picked now stays hidden.
4. Players pass their remaining cards to the left.
5. Repeat steps 2–4 until each hand is down to its last card, after 3 picks. Each player takes the last card passed to them, so everyone drafts 4 cards and nothing is left over.

**Local multiplayer note**: Since all players share one device, a "Pass device to [player name]" screen is shown between picks to keep draft hands private.

### 4. Action Phase

Everyone takes their turn at the same time. Nothing you do on your turn touches anyone else's cards, colors, materials or buyers, so there is no turn order: you can watch the others play, but nothing they do changes what you can do. The round ends once everyone has ended their turn.

On your turn, you may trash any number of your **drafted** cards, one at a time. Each trashed card triggers its trash ability (see Abilities below); a card with two resolves them in the order printed. You must fully resolve a card's abilities before trashing another card. When you are done trashing cards (or choose to stop), your turn ends.

**Spending ducats.** Between trashes — whenever no ability is waiting on you — you may spend **1 ducat** for any of:

| Cost | Buys |
|------|------|
| 1 ducat | Workshop ×1 |
| 1 ducat | Mix Colors ×1 |
| 1 ducat | Sell |

The purchase resolves like any other ability. You may do this as often as you have ducats. A ducat is worth a point at the end of the game, so spending one always costs you a point.

Buying a Sell does not conjure a sale out of nothing: it still spends the material and the colors the sell card asks for, exactly as a drafted Sell card would. What a ducat buys is the *opportunity* — a sale in a round where nothing you drafted lets you make one.

You cannot buy something that would do nothing: Sell needs an affordable buyer, Workshop needs a card in your workshop, and Mix Colors needs two colors on your wheel that can be mixed.

At the end of your turn:
- Every card in your workshop — including the rotated ones already used this round — is shuffled together and placed on the **bottom** of your personal deck.
- Any un-trashed drafted cards move into your workshop, where they are available from the start of the next round.

All player information (workshop, drafted cards, color wheel, stored materials, ducats) is public during the action phase.

## Abilities

When a card is trashed, its trash ability triggers. Abilities are resolved using a **stack (LIFO)** — if an ability triggers further abilities, they are pushed onto the stack and the most recently pushed ability resolves next.

### Workshop ×N

Choose up to N cards from your **workshop**. You may skip Workshop entirely (choosing 0 cards).

For each chosen card:
- **Material card**: Store all materials listed on the card (e.g., 2x Ceramics, or 1 Ceramics + 1 Paintings). If the card has a color, also store that color on your color wheel. The card is rotated and stays in the workshop.
- **Dye or starter dye card**: Store ALL of its colors on your color wheel. The card is rotated and stays in the workshop.
- **Action card**: Consumes 1 pick. The action card's workshop abilities are pushed onto the ability stack, and any remaining Workshop picks carry over (pushed below the workshop abilities on the stack). The card is rotated and stays in the workshop.

### Mix Colors ×N

Perform up to N mix operations on your color wheel. You may stop early. For each mix:
1. Choose two colors that are exactly **2 positions apart** (e.g., Red and Orange, Yellow and Green). Each must be on your wheel, or be a primary you have a wild primary to stand in for.
2. Remove one of each from your wheel, spending a wild primary for any primary you lack.
3. Add one of the color **between** them to your wheel.

Complete mixing pairs (two inputs → one output):
| Input 1 | Input 2 | Output |
|---------|---------|--------|
| Red | Orange | Vermilion |
| Orange | Yellow | Amber |
| Yellow | Green | Chartreuse |
| Green | Blue | Teal |
| Blue | Purple | Ultramarine |
| Purple | Red | Fuchsia |
| Red | Yellow | Orange |
| Yellow | Blue | Green |
| Blue | Red | Purple |

### Move a Card from Workshop to Draft Pool

Choose one card from your **workshop** that has not been workshopped this turn and move it to your **draft pool**. Rotated cards, whose colors and materials have already been stored, cannot be moved. If every card in your workshop is rotated, or it is empty, the ability fizzles.

The moved card is now an ordinary drafted card. You may trash it later in your turn to trigger its ability, exactly as with a card you drafted; triggered abilities are pushed onto the ability stack, so chain reactions are possible. Or you may leave it in the draft pool, in which case end of turn moves it into your workshop instead of onto the bottom of your deck.

### Sell

You **must** sell to one of your own buyers if you are able to. If you cannot afford any of them, the ability fizzles (nothing happens). The shared display cannot be sold to — cards must be claimed in the buyers phase first.

To sell to a buyer:
1. Choose a sell card from your **buyers** section.
2. Spend the stored materials the card asks for, with a boat standing in for each one you lack. A Shipwright also takes a boat of its own.
3. Pay the color cost by removing the required colors from your color wheel, spending a wild primary for each primary you lack.
4. The sell card goes to your **completed sell cards** area.
5. The buyer slot it occupied stays empty until the next buyers phase refills it.

### Gain Ducats ×N

Gain N ducats. Ducats count toward your final score, and can be spent during your turn — see the Action Phase.

### Any Secondary

Choose one of the three secondary colors (Orange, Green, or Purple) and add it to your color wheel. This ability never fizzles.

### Gain Wild Primaries ×N

Gain N wild primaries. See Wild Primaries and Boats above.

### Gain a Boat

Gain 1 boat. See Wild Primaries and Boats above.

## End of Game

The game ends when **round 7** is completed.

Each player's score = the points on their completed sell cards + 1 point for each ducat + ½ point for each wild primary and each boat they still hold. The player with the highest score wins. If tied, the player with the most completed sell cards wins. If still tied, the player with the most colors (total count) on their color wheel wins. If still tied, those players share the victory.
